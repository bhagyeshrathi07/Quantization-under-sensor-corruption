# B. Literature and SOTA Survey Results

**Project:** Quantization-Induced Decision Failures in a Driving Vision-Language Model: A Paired BF16-versus-INT4 Deployment Study  
**Survey date:** September 8, 2026. **Reframed:** September 29, 2026, after instructor feedback narrowed the project to the vision-language strand.  
**Track:** System / Deployment (supported by experimental research)  
**Required strand:** driving vision-language question answering under quantization. The camera-based occupancy perception strand is no longer part of the project; the perception entries below are retained as context and as the record of why that strand was not pursued.

This survey covers ten papers (plus one earlier quantized-driving-VLM citation and three 2026 context items) published or first made public between September 8, 2023 and September 8, 2026. Dates distinguish first preprints from later conference or journal publication. Technical reports are identified as such. Sources were checked through primary papers, official proceedings, and project repositories; the Mathew–Gu study is available through an author-uploaded manuscript and the ICDMW 2025 proceedings record (IEEE Xplore, paywalled). Published results have not been independently reproduced by this team. Statements labeled **project assessment** are our interpretation. Paper descriptions are unchanged from the September 8 survey; the "Role in our project" column and the project assessments were updated on September 29 to reflect the narrowed scope.

**Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct** is the primary planned model, with the contingency defined in [the project proposal](C_PROJECT_PROPOSAL.md). It is selected for a reproducible, bounded deployment study; it is not described as the overall accuracy leader in September 2026.

## 1. FlashOcc: Fast and Memory-Efficient Occupancy Prediction via Channel-to-Height Plugin

**Authors / date:** Zichen Yu et al.; first preprint November 18, 2023. A later conference venue was not verified. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.12058)

FlashOcc reduces the computation required to predict which parts of a three-dimensional scene are occupied. It retains intermediate features in bird's-eye view, uses two-dimensional convolutions, and transforms channels into height at the output. The paper evaluates its accuracy, runtime, and memory tradeoffs on Occ3D-nuScenes. This makes it relevant to deployment experiments that must measure both output quality and actual execution cost. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.12058)

The official repository supplies models, training code, TensorRT instructions, and a linked C++/CUDA implementation. M0 uses a ResNet-50 backbone and one frame. Its reported PyTorch and TensorRT accuracy differ even before INT8 is enabled. **Project assessment (revised):** FlashOcc-M0 was the perception baseline in the September 8 proposal. The strand was removed on instructor advice because implementing a second deployment stack was too much for one semester. The deployment risk we had identified independently supports that decision: the pinned stack (torch 1.10, TensorRT 8.4, CUDA 11.6) predates the RTX 4090, so the custom `bev_pool_v2` plugin would have needed a rebuild before any measurement. The paper remains useful as an example of reporting conversion loss separately from quantization loss, a principle we keep for the VLM strand. [Code and deployment results](https://github.com/Yzichen/FlashOCC)

## 2. Benchmarking and Improving Bird's Eye View Perception Robustness in Autonomous Driving

**Authors / date:** Shaoyuan Xie et al.; first preprint May 27, 2024; published in IEEE TPAMI, vol. 47, no. 5, pp. 3878–3894 (May 2025). This is the expanded RoboBEV study, rather than the original April 2023 preprint. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17426), [journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/TPAMI.2025.3535960)

The study evaluates perception models across detection, segmentation, depth, and occupancy tasks under corruptions and sensor failures. It investigates how pretraining, image-to-BEV transformation, and temporal information relate to robustness, and presents a CLIP-based enhancement. Its findings show why a clean benchmark score is insufficient to describe behavior under degraded inputs. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17426)

The public RoboBEV repository provides corruption-generation and evaluation resources. **Project assessment (revised):** RoboBEV, together with DriveBench (entry 6), is the reason sensor-corruption robustness is now a stress test rather than the primary contribution: corruption benchmarking is established work with released protocols, and a project whose headline is "quantized models under corruption" would add limited new evidence. The same authors' DriveBench corruption realizations are what we use for the VLM stress test. Image color quantization in these suites is an input corruption and differs from reducing model weight precision; we no longer use it. [Official code](https://github.com/worldbench/RoboBEV)

## 3. Boost Embodied AI Models with Robust Compression Boundary

**Authors / date:** Chong Yu, Tao Chen, and Zhongxue Gan (Fudan University); IJCAI 2025 Main Track, pages 6886–6894, DOI 10.24963/ijcai.2025/766; one of three IJCAI 2025 Distinguished Papers, presented August 20, 2025. No arXiv preprint exists. [Official proceedings](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2025/766), [award announcement](https://aihub.org/2025/08/20/congratulations-to-the-ijcai2025-distinguished-paper-award-winners/)

BRCB studies compression while accounting for how embodied models respond to corrupted inputs. The method distinguishes components according to their corruption sensitivity and uses a gating mechanism during compression tuning. Its Table 1 reports FP32 baselines and INT8 compressed variants of BEVFormer, BEVFormer v2, and BEVFusion on nuScenes detection under benign, rainy, snowy, and dark samples; Tables 2–4 extend this to BEV map segmentation and OpenVLA robotics tasks (verified against the proceedings PDF). This is direct evidence that quantized BEV robustness has already been investigated. [Official paper](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2025/0766.pdf)

**Project assessment (revised):** BRCB is the strongest single reason the perception strand had limited novelty: compression of BEV models under corruption already exists as an IJCAI Distinguished Paper. It is retained as evidence for the scope decision and as related work on component-level sensitivity, a principle our optional mitigation also uses. No BRCB implementation was located as of September 8, 2026, so it was never a reproduction dependency.

## 4. PTQAT: A Hybrid Parameter-Efficient Quantization Algorithm for 3D Perception Tasks

**Authors / date:** Xinhao Wang, Zhiwei Lin, Zhongyu Xia, and Yongtao Wang; first preprint August 14, 2025 (v2 August 15, 2025); ICCV 2025 BiVision Workshop; Peking University. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.10557)

PTQAT combines post-training quantization with selective quantization-aware fine-tuning. Rather than updating every quantizable layer, it chooses a subset for training while freezing other layers. The authors evaluate this approach on nuScenes detection, segmentation, and occupancy models. They report that selective tuning can compensate for quantization errors while updating fewer parameters than their QAT comparison. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.10557)

The work distinguishes its weight-only low-bit experiments from a TensorRT W8A8 deployment experiment. That distinction matters: a four-bit accuracy result does not automatically establish executable W4A8 acceleration. **Project assessment (revised):** PTQAT is further evidence that quantized 3D perception is an active area, supporting the decision to drop that strand. Its selective-layer idea is the same principle as our optional mitigation (retaining sensitive decoder layers at BF16), applied to a VLM rather than a perception model, with no fine-tuning. A dedicated official implementation was not located. [Official workshop paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025W/BiVision/papers/Wang_PTQAT_A_Hybrid_Parameter-Efficient_Quantization_Algorithm_for_3D_Perception_Tasks_ICCVW_2025_paper.pdf)

## 5. DriveLM: Driving with Graph Visual Question Answering

**Authors / date:** Chonghao Sima et al.; first preprint December 21, 2023; ECCV 2024 Oral. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.14150)

DriveLM organizes driving questions into a graph that connects perception, prediction, and planning. Instead of treating every answer as unrelated, its task structure represents how recognizing objects can support reasoning about motion and driving decisions. The authors build datasets using nuScenes and CARLA and introduce DriveLM-Agent. Their experiments report benefits when generalizing to unseen objects or sensor configurations. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.14150)

Code and data-preparation scripts are publicly released; checkpoint availability should be confirmed in the repository docs before use. **Project assessment (revised):** DriveLM supplies the perception/prediction/planning task structure that our per-task flip breakdown relies on, and its training scenes are the intended calibration and development source, after removing any overlap with DriveBench final-evaluation scenes. A strong question-answering score remains a limited proxy for driving behavior; it does not demonstrate safe control of a vehicle. Open-ended responses require scoring by meaning rather than exact wording, which is why paraphrases are not counted as answer flips. [Official code and data](https://github.com/OpenDriveLab/DriveLM)

## 6. Are VLMs Ready for Autonomous Driving? An Empirical Study from the Reliability, Data, and Metric Perspectives

**Authors / date:** Shaoyuan Xie et al.; first preprint January 7, 2025; ICCV 2025. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.04003), [official code and publication record](https://github.com/worldbench/DriveBench)

This paper introduces DriveBench, which tests VLMs with clean images, corrupted images, and absent visual information. It studies four driving tasks and separate corruption-awareness questions. Its central finding is that plausible responses can reflect textual cues or general knowledge even when useful image evidence is absent. Dataset imbalance and evaluation choices can conceal this behavior. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.04003)

DriveBench provides public evaluation code and data resources. Its core driving table uses perception MCQ/VQA, prediction VQA, planning VQA, and behavior MCQ. Captioning appears in the separate corruption-awareness study. **Project assessment (revised):** DriveBench is the required evaluation framework. We use its core driving cells, its per-question camera-view selection (which gives us the view-count factor), and three of its released conditions (Dark, Fog, NoImage) as the stress test. Its finding that models can answer plausibly without image evidence is directly relevant: the NoImage control lets us check whether a flip depends on the image at all. We audit skipped questions and mark unavailable cells N/A. Our extension is the paired BF16/INT4 flip analysis on top of this framework, not a new benchmark. [Paper, Tables 6–7](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.04003v1#A1), [released evaluator](https://github.com/worldbench/DriveBench/blob/main/evaluate/eval.py)

## 7. Qwen2.5-VL Technical Report

**Authors / date:** Shuai Bai et al.; first preprint February 19, 2025. This is a technical report; no conference venue is claimed. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.13923)

Qwen2.5-VL combines a dynamic-resolution vision transformer with a language model. Its design includes window attention and video time information, supporting image understanding, object localization, and video tasks. The report evaluates broad multimodal capabilities across model sizes. Its largest-model results should not be treated as measured performance of the 7B model or evidence of driving reliability. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.13923)

The team releases a 7B instruction-tuned checkpoint and official AWQ variants. **Project assessment (revised):** Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct is our single required model because it offers a modern visual backbone, an official AWQ reference recipe (which quantizes the language decoder and leaves the vision encoder at BF16), and vLLM support. We will hold the image-token limit, decoding settings, and prompt template fixed across precisions. Fitting the complete multi-image workload in 24 GB must be demonstrated in the pilot; the 3B model is the fallback. Qwen2.5-VL is a reproducibility choice, not a claim about the latest model ranking. [Model card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct), [official release history](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-VL#news)

## 8. Qwen3-VL Technical Report

**Authors / date:** Shuai Bai et al.; first preprint November 26, 2025. This is a technical report. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.21631)

Qwen3-VL updates the model family through revised spatial-temporal position encoding, integration of features from several vision-transformer layers, and explicit video timestamp alignment. It offers multiple dense and mixture-of-experts sizes. The authors report improvements across multimodal evaluations and discuss quality under token and latency constraints. These claims concern their reported workloads, rather than our proposed driving experiment. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.21631)

The official repository links released models, including the 4B and 8B variants (released October 15, 2025, before the technical report), and provides inference examples. **Project assessment (revised):** this paper establishes newer architectural context and prevents us from describing Qwen2.5-VL as the newest Qwen vision-language model. A small Qwen3-VL variant is the natural second-model extension for testing whether the flip pattern transfers across a model generation. Any such comparison must pair each model with its own BF16 reference; changing model generation together with precision would mix architecture, training, and quantization effects. [Official code and models](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-VL)

## 9. Can Small Quantized VLMs Drive? An Experimental Evaluation of Small Quantized VLMs for Autonomous Driving

**Authors / date:** Samson Mathew and Zonghua Gu (Hofstra University); 2025 IEEE International Conference on Data Mining Workshops (ICDMW), Washington, DC, November 12–15, 2025, pages 3255–3262, DOI 10.1109/ICDMW69685.2025.00440 (added to IEEE Xplore March 10, 2026). The official program lists the paper in the ICDM 2025 Undergraduate and High School Symposium (oral session UH1, November 15, 2025); it is cited here as an empirical data point, not as a peer-reviewed main-track result. The team holds the published PDF; the claims above were verified against it. [Publication DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICDMW69685.2025.00440), [official conference program](https://www3.cs.stonybrook.edu/~icdm2025/program.html)

This study evaluates nine general-purpose VLMs (SmolVLM 256M/500M/2.2B, Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B-Instruct, Gemma-3 4B/12B, LLaVA-1.5 7B/13B) at FP16 and as 4-bit BitsAndBytes variants, 18 configurations in total, served through vLLM on DriveBench (Table I and Sections II-B and III of the paper). Answers are scored 0–100 by GPT-5-mini. The experiments cover clean, corrupted, and missing-image conditions, answer quality, and inference timing. Reported latency benefits are mixed, showing why reduced weight precision cannot substitute for runtime measurement. The main quality summaries aggregate across driving tasks. [Author-uploaded manuscript](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/396983942_Can_Small_Quantized_VLMs_Drive_An_Experimental_Evaluation_of_Small_Quantized_VLMs_for_Autonomous_Driving)

**Project assessment (revised): this is the main comparison for the project.** Mathew and Gu already quantize Qwen2.5-VL-7B to 4 bits, serve it in vLLM, and evaluate it on DriveBench, so none of those steps is our novelty. Their conclusion, that the 4-bit gap is "not very large" and some 4-bit variants score slightly higher than FP16, is an aggregate statement. Our project asks what that aggregate hides: it pairs every question across precisions, counts harmful and beneficial flips separately, and breaks them down by task, format, confidence margin, view count, and condition. Three differences from their design matter: (1) they use BitsAndBytes 4-bit, a runtime dequantization path, whereas we use AWQ INT4 with a vLLM-native kernel, so memory and latency results are not comparable across studies; (2) they score every answer 0–100 with an LLM judge, whereas we use exact MCQ accuracy as the primary flip signal; (3) they report no per-question pairing, margin, or view-count analysis. A public experiment repository was not located. The paper's introduction says "eight small-scale VLMs" while Table I lists nine. An earlier data point exists: EM-VLM4AD (Gopalkrishnan, Greer, and Trivedi; CVPR 2024 Workshop on Vision and Language for Autonomous Driving and Robotics; [arXiv:2403.19838](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.19838), [code](https://github.com/akshaygopalkr/EM-VLM4AD)) evaluated an 8-bit quantized T5-Large driving VLM on DriveLM, so quantized driving VQA predates Mathew–Gu; neither work analyzes per-question decision changes.

## 10. MBQ: Modality-Balanced Quantization for Large Vision-Language Models

**Authors / date:** Shiyao Li et al.; first preprint December 27, 2024 (v2 March 21, 2025); CVPR 2025, pages 4167–4177. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19509)

MBQ examines differences between the quantization sensitivity of visual and language tokens. It adjusts calibration so that reconstruction objectives account for those differences, rather than weighting all modalities equally. The paper studies weight-only and weight-and-activation quantization, and separately demonstrates a specialized low-bit GPU kernel. This connects calibration choices to accuracy while distinguishing a numerical quantization experiment from an executable speed demonstration. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19509)

The official implementation includes quantization search and evaluation code. Its documented model choices include Qwen2-VL and several other families; turnkey Qwen2.5-VL support was not verified. Several example evaluation commands use pseudo quantization, and the W3 GPU kernel reported in the paper is not included in the public repository. **Project assessment (revised):** MBQ establishes that quantization sensitivity differs across components of a VLM, which motivates both our margin analysis and the optional mitigation of retaining selected decoder layers at BF16. It also shows that sensitivity-aware calibration is occupied territory, so our mitigation is a controlled evaluation of an existing principle, not a new algorithm. Adapting MBQ itself is optional. [Official code](https://github.com/thu-nics/MBQ)

## Synthesis and implications for the proposal

The literature supports the importance of the problem and also establishes substantial overlap. Corruption benchmarking of perception models (RoboBEV) and of driving VLMs (DriveBench) is established; compression of BEV models under corruption exists (BRCB, PTQAT); quantized driving-VLM evaluation exists (EM-VLM4AD, Mathew–Gu); and sensitivity-aware VLM quantization exists (MBQ, QIG). Adjacent 2025–2026 work tests precision × input-degradation interactions on 2D detection (Karimov et al., [arXiv:2508.19600](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19600)) and on non-driving VLM reliability (Ferdous, [arXiv:2607.24440](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24440)). Together these are why, on September 29, 2026, the project dropped the perception strand and demoted sensor corruption from primary question to stress test.

**Our candidate contribution is a paired, per-question failure analysis of one quantized driving VLM on named hardware:** which BF16-correct answers does INT4 change, how many of those changes are harmful to the driving decision, and do task, answer format, BF16 confidence margin, view count, or corruption predict them, alongside the measured memory and latency the deployment buys. None of the inspected works reports per-question flips, margin, or view-count breakdowns for a quantized driving VLM. This is a proposed extension, not a first-of-its-kind claim.

The minimum implementation is one VLM at two precisions on DriveBench, with the 3B contingency if the 7B pilot fails. Adding architectures, all precision formats, custom kernels, a new benchmark, or reproductions of methods without released code is outside the minimum commitment. A finding that flips are rare and unpredictable is still useful if its uncertainty is small enough to bound the harmful-flip rate; it does not establish safe autonomous driving.

## Latest-context check beyond the ten selected entries

The main survey is intentionally limited to ten substantive entries. Three additional 2026 developments inform its limitations:

- **QIG (Xiang et al., CVPR 2026; [arXiv:2603.17809](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17809), March 18, 2026)** estimates token-level quantization sensitivity with integrated gradients; official code and evaluation pipeline released April 3, 2026, documenting InternVL2, LLaVA-OneVision, and Qwen2-VL. It reinforces that sensitivity-aware quantization is active prior art; its documented paths still require compatibility checks for our selected deployment. [Official repository](https://github.com/ucas-xiang/QIG)
- **Less Precise Can Be More Reliable (ICML 2026; [arXiv:2509.21173](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.21173), revised May 15, 2026)** evaluates quantization's effect on CLIP-style VLM reliability, including robustness to noise. Its setting differs from generative driving question answering, but it is further evidence that quantization robustness beyond average accuracy is already studied. Its reliability framing (calibration, confidence) is the closest existing work to our margin analysis, on a different model class and task.
- **PeLiC-VLM (Gan et al., *Advanced Engineering Informatics* 71:104424, April 2026)**, "Performance and lightweight coexistence: Vision-language model for visual scene question answering in autonomous driving," combines a multi-view aggregation network, a visual refinement module, and an efficient quantized image-text adapter (268 M parameters, 4.7 GFLOPs, 0.49 GB). Its publisher abstract was available, but full-text access was blocked during this survey. Whether it already reports paired precision losses by task is **unverified**, and remains an explicit overlap question. [Publisher record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1474034626001163), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aei.2026.104424)

This is a targeted recent-literature survey, not an exhaustive proof that no overlapping paper exists. The team will refresh the search (in particular for "quantization" together with "answer flips", "decision flips", "prediction churn", or "confidence margin" on VLMs) before freezing its final contribution claims and will report baseline reproductions under pinned software, checkpoint, dataset, and evaluation versions.
