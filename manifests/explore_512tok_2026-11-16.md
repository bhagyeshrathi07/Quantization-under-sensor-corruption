# Exploratory check: how much the frozen 128-token cap truncates VQA answers

November 16, 2026. Exploratory, outside the core freeze; nothing here is study data.

## Why

While building the labelling tool, the first sheet item showed a model answer ending mid-bullet. The development records confirm it: generation `max_tokens` is 128 (core freeze, PROTOCOL section 4), and 260 of the 262 VQA answers on the label sheet (all perception and planning answers, 20 of 22 prediction answers) stop at exactly 128 completion tokens. The released DriveBench decoding uses 512 tokens. The 128 came from the October 1 pilot skeleton, which was built around MCQ answers read from the first token, and was carried into plan section 5.1 and the November 6 freeze without anyone checking VQA completion lengths. The judge scores the same cut-off text for both precisions, so the comparison is a fair one under 128 tokens, but it describes behaviour under that cap only: the two precisions may reach the relevant content at different positions, so nothing about it need carry over to 512. Absolute VQA scores are not comparable to the paper's, and the human labels would mostly see answers that never reach their point. The final matrix is the same: 4,551 of its 4,576 VQA answers (99.5%) stop at 128.

## What was run

`config/explore_512tok.yaml` is the pilot config with `generation.max_tokens` 512 and nothing else changed (freeze check on `config/pilot.yaml` unaffected). On the GPU host: BF16 server, `03_smoke_drivebench.py --n 60 --split development --condition clean --config config/explore_512tok.yaml --out-dir results/explore_512tok`, then INT4 with the own checkpoint. 60 stratified development questions per precision (12 per cell; the prediction cell has 11), 0 errors, 23:17 to 23:25 UTC, about 8 minutes of GPU time. The sidecars record `max_tokens` 512 and no `freeze_checked`: `05` and `08` accept these files as exploratory development data (and say so), the calibration tools refuse them, and nothing from them can be frozen. `results/explore_512tok/` is not tracked; `logs/explore_512tok.txt` is the driver log.

## Result

Same 35 VQA questions per precision, at 512 versus the frozen 128:

| precision | cell | n | at cap, 512 | median tokens, 512 | ends on a sentence, 512 | at cap, 128 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| BF16 | perception/VQA | 12 | 0 | 235.5 | 12 | 12 |
| BF16 | planning/VQA | 12 | 0 | 431 | 12 | 12 |
| BF16 | prediction/VQA | 11 | 1 | 357 | 10 | 9 |
| INT4 | perception/VQA | 12 | 0 | 267 | 12 | 12 |
| INT4 | planning/VQA | 12 | 0 | 406.5 | 12 | 12 |
| INT4 | prediction/VQA | 11 | 2 | 416 | 8 | 11 |

At 512, 67 of 70 answers stay below the cap (the three at it are prediction answers that walk through all six camera views before answering); the records carry no finish reason, so "below the cap" is what is known, not "complete" (`finish_reason` is recorded from now on). At 128 nearly none does. All 24 planning answers exceed 256 tokens, so 256 would still cut most of them. Longer answers are not automatically better answers: they can add errors and contradictions, so the 128-token scores are not lower bounds of anything.

## Decision (November 16, reviewer's pass; team confirmation pending)

Redo the VQA generation at 512 tokens before any human labelling, as a separate VQA amendment. The MCQ study, its core freeze and every historical result stay exactly as they are; the five MCQ contrasts are unchanged. VQA remains the exploratory, secondary measure it always was.

- A new, enforced VQA generation freeze (decoding `max_tokens` 512, everything else equal to the core freeze), beside the core freeze, with its own check in the runner and in every analysis path that reads VQA answers.
- VQA answers generated into separate directories, never over the existing files: 262 development answers (131 questions per precision) and 4,576 final answers (572 per run, 8 runs). The MCQ rows of the existing files stay the MCQ evidence.
- Budget from the measured latencies at 512 weighted by the actual cell counts: 5.44 GPU hours for the final matrix plus 0.31 for the development run, 5.75 hours before server starts, assuming the corrupted conditions produce answers of similar length to clean (about $5 at the current rate). The existing latency and throughput measurements describe the 128-token protocol and say nothing about this run.
- Development judged again: 786 fresh calls; the judging cost note is refreshed with the measured longer inputs before the final judging is budgeted. Replacement blind sheets issued from the new development answers (the November 14 sheets are withdrawn unlabelled); the labelling guide and the calibration acceptance rules apply unchanged.
- Only after acceptance and the judge freeze: final judging, 13,728 calls for three replicates.
- Lesson for the methods section: check completion lengths against the cap before freezing a decoding setting; the `completion_tokens` field in every record is what made this check possible after the fact, and `finish_reason` is recorded from now on.

## Implementation (November 16, after the reviewer's third pass)

PROTOCOL section 15 describes the mechanics; the command sequence is in `judge_decisions_2026-11-13.md`. In short: `config/vqa512.yaml` (pilot config, `max_tokens` 512), `scripts/freeze_config.py --amendment vqa512` writes `manifests/config_frozen_vqa512.yaml` on the core freeze and refuses any other difference, `03 --amendment vqa512` runs the VQA cells only under it and records it in the sidecar, `AMENDMENT=vqa512` drives the matrix into `results/final_vqa512/`, and `05`, `08`, `10`, `11` take `--amendment vqa512`, check the amendment against the core freeze, and refuse rows outside the VQA cells. Tests in `tests/test_amendment.py` (the allowed difference alone, inherited environment, stale base refused, run guard identity, analysis and calibration acceptance on either side, cell filter, the real config against the real core freeze).
